Operations & Client Experience

How projects run from first hello to launch day. Checklists, call scripts, email templates, and project management workflows live here, ready to open during real projects. Plus your sales strategy and financial projections.

Client Onboarding Process

Your onboarding process has four clear phases. Phase 1: Lead to Qualified Prospect is handled by your partner. A lead comes in (ad, referral, website form, cold outreach), and your partner qualifies them on budget (can afford $500+ minimum?), timeline (realistic expectations of 2–4 weeks?), fit (target market local GTA business?), and authority (decision maker or influencer?). If qualified, partner books discovery call. If not, partner politely declines or refers elsewhere.

Phase 2: Discovery Call (30 minutes) includes your marketing partner leading, you joining for technical questions, and the prospect. The agenda covers introductions (2 min), understanding business goals (5 min), current pain points (5 min), reviewing service needs (8 min), technical questions (5 min), pricing discussion (3 min), and next steps (2 min). After the call, partner sends follow-up email with proposed package or custom quote, timeline estimate, portfolio examples, and clear CTA.

Phase 3: Proposal & Closing is partner-led. Partner sends formal proposal, client reviews (give 3–5 days), partner answers questions (loops you in for technical), partner handles price negotiation (consults you on feasibility), and once agreed, partner sends contract, client signs electronically, and 50% deposit invoice is sent immediately.

Phase 4: Project Kickoff is triggered by deposit payment received. Within 24 hours you send client welcome email, send intake questionnaire (Google Form), schedule kickoff call (30 min, within 3 days), create project folder (Google Drive), add project to Trello/Notion, and set calendar reminders for milestones.

Kickoff Call Script

Use this script for every kickoff call — it ensures you don't forget critical details. The call is 30 minutes with you, client, and partner (optional).

Introduction (3 min): "Hi [Client], great to officially get started! Today we'll walk through exactly what I need from you, confirm the timeline, and answer any questions. I've got 30 minutes — does that work?" Quick intro (who you are, your process), confirm they reviewed/signed the proposal, set expectation: working call, you'll take notes together.

Scope Confirmation (5 min): "Let's make sure we're 100% aligned on what we're building." Review deliverables: number of pages (list them), features (forms, booking, maps, e-commerce), SEO included (yes/no), content writing included (yes/no), Google Business Profile (yes/no). Confirm timeline: [X business days] = [TARGET DATE]. Clarify: unlimited minor revisions for 30 days. Questions to ask: "Any features we haven't discussed yet?" "Any must-haves I should prioritize?" "Anything to absolutely avoid?"

Design Preferences (7 min): "Let's talk about the look and feel." Competitor analysis: "Who are your main competitors online?" "What do you like about their websites?" "What DON'T you like?" Design inspiration: "I sent a questionnaire asking for 3 example sites you like. Did you get a chance to look?" (If not: show 2–3 examples and ask which they prefer) "What specifically appeals to you? Colors? Layout? Vibe?" Brand guidelines: Logo (do you have high-res?), colors (brand colors or should I propose palette?), fonts (existing fonts or want recommendations?), imagery (what vibe? professional, friendly, bold, minimal). Questions to ask: "How should visitors FEEL when they land on your site?" "One word that describes your brand?" "Any colors you absolutely hate?"

Content & Assets (8 min): "Let's talk about text, images, videos for your site." Content responsibility matrix: Logo (client gives), business description (client gives or pay), service details (client gives or pay), team photos (client gives), work photos (client gives), stock photos (you handle if in package), testimonials (client gives), contact info (client gives). Deadlines: Logo plus brand colors (within 3 days), text content (within 7 days), photos (within 7 days). Questions to ask: "Do you have professional photos of your work/team/products?" "Do you have customer testimonials I can use?" "What are your top 3 services/products?" "What should visitors DO on your site? (call, email, book, buy)"

Technical Setup (4 min): "Quick boring but important technical stuff." Domain and hosting: Do you own a domain? (If yes: will need login access OR guide you through DNS later. If no: you'll send buying guide, ~$15/year, client pays.) Do you have hosting? (If yes: need login to install WordPress. If no: you'll recommend SiteGround/Hostinger, ~$10/mo, client pays.) Email: Do you have business email? (If no: want one? Google Workspace $8/mo, client pays.) Access and accounts: Google Business Profile (have one? If yes: you'll need manager access. If no: you'll set it up if in package). Google Analytics (want tracking? Yes = you'll set it up).

Timeline & Communication (2 min): "Here's how we'll stay in touch." Timeline: Design preview ([DATE — 5–7 days]), full site preview ([DATE — 10–12 days]), revisions ([DATE — 2–3 days]), launch ([DATE — 14–16 days total]). Communication: Email updates every 3–4 days, Loom video walkthroughs (easier than screenshots), client can email you anytime, you respond within 24 hours (weekdays), urgent issues: text your number (site down only).

Q&A (1 min): "What questions do you have?" (Pause. Let them ask. If silent:) "Clear on what I need from you?" "Does the timeline work?" "Anything concerning you?"

Next Steps (1 min): "Here's what happens next:" Today (you): you'll send Google Form for content/assets. Within 3 days (client): fill out form with what you have. In 5–7 days (you): design preview via Loom video. In 10–12 days (you): full site preview (staging site). In 14–16 days: launch!

Post-call checklist (you, within 1 hour): Send recap email (meeting notes, action items, deadlines, intake form link), create project folder (Google Drive), add to Trello/Notion with checklist, set calendar reminders, start competitor research (if not done).

Project Management

Use a Trello board called "vaire* digital — Client Projects" with these lists: Leads (partner manages), Quoted (waiting on deposit), Kickoff (deposit received, gathering requirements), Design (creating mockups, client review), Development (building site), Review (client testing, revisions), Launch Prep (pre-launch QA checklist), Launched (live plus 7-day monitoring), Archived (project complete).

Each card template includes: Title ([CLIENT NAME] — [PROJECT TYPE]), labels (Package: Starter/Growth/Premium/Custom, Status: On Track/At Risk/Blocked, Priority: High/Normal/Low), checklist for project milestones, attachments (link to project folder, contract/proposal, staging site, live site), due date (target launch date), and comments (log all updates here with dates).

Email Templates

You have copy-paste ready email templates for project kickoff recap, design preview, final review before launch, and launch day. Each email clearly sets expectations, tells the client what to review or deliver, and signals next steps so projects keep moving without confusion. These templates are in your operational systems folder and can be customized per client.

Pre-Launch QA Checklist

Use this checklist for every project before launch. It covers functionality (all links work, forms submit, no JavaScript errors, no 404 errors), content (no placeholder text, proper heading hierarchy, correct contact info), SEO (unique meta tags, sitemap submitted, schema markup, Google Analytics), performance (page load speed under 3 seconds, Lighthouse score over 85, images optimized), mobile responsiveness (test on iPhone, Android, iPad, navigation works, no horizontal scrolling), browser testing (Chrome, Firefox, Safari, Edge, mobile browsers), security (SSL certificate, strong passwords, security plugin), backups (backup plugin installed, initial backup completed, schedule set), compliance (Privacy Policy page, cookie notice, AODA accessibility check), and client handoff (training call scheduled, login credentials prepared, tutorial videos recorded).

Sales & Client Acquisition

Lead generation channels in priority order: referrals (highest ROI, warmest leads), local SEO (long-term, scalable), content marketing (authority building), social media (awareness and social proof), paid ads (optional, budget-dependent), and cold outreach (last resort, partner-led).

Your role in sales: join discovery calls (30 min) for technical questions, provide realistic timelines and feasibility checks, answer technical questions prospects ask, showcase portfolio and explain your process, create sales collateral (portfolio, case studies, one-pagers). Your partner's role: lead generation (ads, outreach, networking), initial contact with leads, lead qualification, lead discovery calls, price negotiation, closing deals, follow-up emails, contract sending.

Sales collateral includes portfolio/case studies (3–5 minimum), one-page service menu (PDF), and proposal template. You need portfolio pieces before you can effectively sell — either real discounted projects ($200–400 for 60–80% discount) or practice projects (fictional but professional).

Objection handling: "Too expensive" → "Most agencies charge $3,000–5,000 for this. We're at $1,200 because we use efficient tools. If budget is tight, we could start with a 3-page landing page for $500, then add more later. Would that work?" "I can use Wix for $20/month" → "You could! The difference is time and expertise. DIY takes 20–30 hours to learn. Plus we handle SEO, speed optimization, and lead generation — not just pretty design. What's your time worth?" "I want to think about it" → "Totally fair. Can I ask what specifically you're unsure about? I'll send you [case study/testimonial] so you can see results we've delivered. When's a good time to follow up?" "Need to talk to my partner" → "Makes sense! Would it help if I joined that conversation to answer technical questions? Or I can send a one-pager summarizing everything. Which is better?" "Why you over [competitor]?" → "We're different in 3 ways: (1) Local — same timezone, understand GTA market. (2) Transparent — no jargon, clear pricing, you own everything. (3) Fast — AI tools let us deliver in half the time. Most agencies take 4–6 weeks; we're done in 2."

Financial Projections

Your 12-month revenue roadmap (conservative estimate) assumes 50/50 partnership split on all revenue, mix of projects and retainers, gradual ramp as portfolio builds. Month 1: 1 project (portfolio build, discounted), $400 gross, $200 your share. Month 2: 2 projects (1 portfolio plus 1 landing page), $900 gross, $450 your share. Month 3: 3 projects (2 landing plus 1 Starter package), $1,700 gross, $850 your share. Month 4: 3 projects (1 Starter plus 1 Growth plus 1 landing), $2,800 gross, $1,400 your share. Month 5: 4 projects (2 Starter plus 1 Growth plus 1 SEO), $4,200 gross, $2,100 your share. Month 6: 4 projects (2 Starter plus 1 Growth plus 1 Premium) plus 2 retainers start, $5,600 gross, $2,800 your share. This continues growing through Month 12.

Year 1 totals: Gross revenue approximately $72,000, your share (50%) approximately $36,000. Project revenue approximately $63,000 gross (your share: $31,500), retainer revenue approximately $9,000 gross (your share: $4,500), average monthly (Year 1) $3,000 per month to you. Key milestones: Month 3 first $850+ month (target: $1,000), Month 6 break $2,500/month, Month 9 break $4,000/month, Month 12 break $6,000/month. This is conservative — if your partner is a strong closer or you get referral momentum, you could exceed this by 50–100%.

Startup costs (one-time): Domain for your site ($15–20 annual), hosting Vercel ($0 free tier), business cards ($30–50 optional), logo design ($0–150), legal/business registration ($60–100), accounting software ($0 Wave free). Total startup: $105–320.

Monthly operating costs: Domain (amortized ~$1.50), hosting ($0 Vercel free tier), business email ($0–8), Cursor/Claude Pro ($0–20), software ($0 use free tiers), marketing/ads ($0–100 partner may handle), phone ($0 use personal line), insurance ($0–50 defer until revenue). Total monthly: $10–180. Your monthly costs can be as low as $10–30 if you use all free tiers and defer insurance/paid tools.

Break-even analysis: Monthly costs $10–30 (minimal) or $50–100 (with paid tools). Break-even: 1 landing page project ($500 = $250 to you) covers 2–8 months of expenses. You're profitable from Month 1 if you land even 1 discounted project.

Effective hourly rate tracking: Track your time per project to understand profitability. Example: Project $1,200 Starter Package, your share (50%) $600, your time 25 hours, effective rate $600 ÷ 25 = $24/hour. Target effective rates: Months 1–3 $15–20/hour (learning, building portfolio), Months 4–6 $20–30/hour (getting faster), Months 7–12 $30–40/hour (efficient systems), Year 2+ $40–60/hour (expertise premium). As you get faster with AI tools and templates, your effective hourly rate increases even if prices stay the same.
