Chapter 5

Risks, Protection & Growth Paths

What can go wrong and how you'll handle it. Common pitfalls, insurance and legal protection, and four different paths to scale in Year 2. Written to be read together over coffee, not scanned like a spreadsheet.

Common Risks & Mitigation Strategies

Risk 1: Not Enough Clients

Likelihood: High (Months 1–3). Impact: High (no revenue).

Mitigation: Build 3 portfolio pieces BEFORE officially launching. Offer discounted projects to friends/family/network. Partner focuses on lead generation while you build portfolio. Have 6 months of personal expenses saved (if possible). Keep part-time job until Month 3–6.

Early Warning Signs: Month 3: Less than 2 inquiries per week. Month 6: Less than 2 projects closed per month.

Action Plan: Adjust pricing (temporarily lower if needed), increase partner's outreach efforts, run limited-time promotions, expand service area (beyond GTA if needed).

Risk 2: Projects Take Longer Than Expected

Likelihood: High (Months 1–6, you're learning). Impact: Medium (low effective hourly rate, unhappy clients).

Mitigation: Use AI tools aggressively (Claude Code, ChatGPT). Create templates and reusable components. Add 30% buffer to your internal timeline estimates. Be honest with clients about being a junior dev (builds trust). Under-promise, over-deliver on timelines.

Early Warning Signs: Projects consistently taking 2x your estimate, clients asking "When will it be done?", you're working 50+ hours/week and falling behind.

Action Plan: Analyze where time is going (track meticulously), automate or template repetitive tasks, ask Claude Code to help optimize your workflow, consider raising prices to reduce volume (more time per project), pause new projects until current ones are complete.

Risk 3: Scope Creep

Likelihood: High (especially with new clients). Impact: Medium (reduces profitability, delays).

Mitigation: Very clear scope in proposal and contract. "Unlimited minor revisions" clearly defined (text edits, image swaps). Major changes equal new quote (in writing). Use project management tool (Trello) to track approved scope. Politely but firmly say "That's outside the current scope, but I can quote it separately."

Early Warning Signs: Client asking for "just one more page", revisions expanding beyond minor changes, requests for features not in original proposal.

Action Plan: Reference the original proposal: "In our agreement we specified [X]. What you're asking for is [Y], which I'm happy to do for an additional $[amount]." Document everything in email (paper trail). Be kind but firm. Offer options: add to current project (pay more) OR add after launch (separate project).

Risk 4: Non-Paying Clients

Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High (waste of time, loss of income).

Mitigation: ALWAYS get 50% deposit before starting. Deposits are non-refundable (in contract). Final 50% due before site goes live on their domain. Keep site on staging subdomain until paid. Use payment processors (Stripe) for card-on-file. Clear payment terms in contract (Net 15).

Early Warning Signs: Client delays paying deposit, client doesn't respond to final invoice, client disputes work quality after completion.

Action Plan: Follow payment policy strictly. Take site offline if payment not received by Day 45. Small claims court if necessary (for amounts over $1,000). Learn from it: tighten contract, better client qualification.

Risk 5: Technical Issues

Likelihood: Low–Medium. Impact: High (reputation damage, client trust).

Mitigation: Security plugin on every site (Wordfence). Weekly automated backups (UpdraftPlus to Google Drive). Test backup restoration (at least once). SSL certificate on every site (HTTPS). Strong passwords, limit login attempts. Update WordPress, themes, plugins regularly. Uptime monitoring (UptimeRobot free).

Early Warning Signs: Client reports site is slow or down, Wordfence alerts about suspicious activity, Google flags site as "unsafe".

Action Plan: Immediate response (within 2 hours for critical issues). Restore from backup if site compromised. Scan with Wordfence, clean malware. Change all passwords. Notify client immediately and honestly. If your fault: offer refund or free month of maintenance.

Risk 6: Partner Relationship Issues

Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High (business could collapse).

Mitigation: Clear written agreement on revenue split (50/50). Define roles clearly (you build, they sell). Regular check-ins (weekly 30-min calls). Transparent communication on financials. Separate business bank account (split deposits). Written partnership agreement (even if informal initially).

Early Warning Signs: Disagreements on pricing or scope, one partner not pulling their weight, money disputes, poor communication.

Action Plan: Address issues immediately (don't let resentment build). Revisit agreement (is it still fair as business grows?). Consider mediation if serious conflict. Have exit clause in partnership agreement (how to split if it ends).

Insurance & Legal Protection

Professional Liability Insurance (Errors & Omissions): What it covers: mistakes, missed deadlines, negligence claims. Cost: $300–600/year for $1M coverage. When to get: before your 5th client or $10K in revenue. Recommended providers: Simply Business, Hiscox, Thimble.

General Liability Insurance: What it covers: bodily injury, property damage (less relevant for web dev). Cost: $200–400/year. When to get: if meeting clients in person or at their location. Often bundled with professional liability.

Cyber Liability Insurance: What it covers: data breaches, cyber attacks on client sites. Cost: $500–1,000/year. When to get: if handling sensitive client data (e-commerce, medical).

Recommendation: Start with professional liability only once you hit $10K revenue or 5 clients. Defer others until Year 2.

Legal Contracts: Client Service Agreement (Contract) should cover scope of work, payment terms, revision policy, ownership of work, limitation of liability, termination clause. Where to get: Law Depot (Canada-specific templates, ~$50), Legal Zoom, local lawyer (consultation plus custom contract: $500–1,000). Start with template from Law Depot, have lawyer review once you can afford it.

Scaling Strategy (Months 12–24)

Month 12 Targets: 35–45 projects completed (Year 1 total), 6–8 retainer clients, $60,000–75,000 gross revenue, $30,000–37,500 your share, effective hourly rate: $30–40/hour.

Scaling Path A: Increase Volume

Take on more projects (6–8 per month). Hire contractor or junior dev to help with delivery. You become project manager plus quality control. Partner continues to handle all sales. Pros: more revenue, predictable income. Cons: still trading time for money, capped by your hours. Revenue potential Year 2: $80,000–120,000 gross.

Scaling Path B: Increase Prices

Raise prices by 30–50% (you've proven value). Focus on fewer, higher-value clients. Add premium services (e-commerce, custom apps). Position as "premium" vs "affordable". Pros: higher margins, less work, better clients. Cons: slower growth, smaller market. Revenue potential Year 2: $90,000–130,000 gross.

Scaling Path C: Recurring Revenue Focus

Shift focus to retainer services (maintenance, SEO, GBP). Target: 20–30 retainer clients by end of Year 2. Reduce one-time projects (or charge more for them). Build semi-passive income. Pros: predictable income, less sales effort, scalable. Cons: takes time to build, requires excellent service. Revenue potential Year 2: $50,000–80,000 gross (but recurring!).

Scaling Path D: Productize & Automate

Create "website-in-a-box" templates by industry. Use page builders plus AI for ultra-fast delivery. Reduce custom work, increase templated work. Aim for 8–12 sites per month with templates. Pros: ultra-fast delivery, very profitable. Cons: less creative, commodity pricing pressure. Revenue potential Year 2: $120,000–180,000 gross.

Recommended: Hybrid of B plus C — raise prices gradually (15–20% every 6 months), build retainer base to 15–20 clients (recurring income), take on fewer but higher-value projects. Year 2 target: $100,000–140,000 gross revenue.

When & How to Hire

DON'T hire too early. Common mistake. When to consider hiring: you're consistently turning away work (good problem), you're working 50+ hours/week for 3+ months, you have 10+ retainer clients (need support), revenue is $8,000+/month consistently (can afford it).

Who to hire first: Option 1: Junior Developer (Part-Time Contractor) — role: build sites under your direction, cost: $20–30/hour CAD, 10–20 hours/week, when: you're booked solid with projects. Option 2: Virtual Assistant (VA) — role: client communication, scheduling, admin tasks, cost: $15–25/hour CAD, 10–15 hours/week, when: you're spending over 10 hours/week on admin. Option 3: SEO Specialist (Contractor) — role: handle all SEO work (you just build sites), cost: $25–40/hour CAD or revenue share, when: you have 5+ SEO retainer clients.

Recommendation: Start with VA to free up your time, then hire dev if volume justifies it.
