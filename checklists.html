Business Launch Checklist

Before officially launching vaire* digital, ensure you've completed these items:

Business Foundation: Business name decided (vaire* digital or other), business registered (or plan to register after first few clients), partnership agreement with marketing partner (written, even informal), revenue split confirmed (50/50), roles clarified (you: build, partner: sell), business bank account opened (or plan for separate account).

Legal & Admin: Client contract template acquired (Law Depot or lawyer), proposal template created, invoice template created (Wave or Google Sheets), payment terms decided (50% deposit, 50% on completion), refund policy written, Privacy Policy page drafted (for your site).

Technical Setup: LocalWP installed and working, Cursor IDE installed with Claude Code access, GitHub account created, domain purchased for your agency site (yourname.ca), business email set up (Google Workspace or free Zoho), Google Analytics account created, Google Search Console account created.

Your Agency Website: Next.js site built (or WordPress if you prefer), homepage with clear value proposition, services page with 3 packages plus pricing, portfolio page with 3–5 case studies, contact form working (tested), SEO basics done (meta tags, sitemap, analytics), mobile-responsive, deployed to Vercel and live on your domain.

Portfolio Pieces (Minimum 3): Portfolio project #1 complete (fictional or real), portfolio project #2 complete, portfolio project #3 complete, screenshots taken for each project, case studies written (problem, solution, results), testimonials received (if real clients) or prepared (if fictional).

Sales Collateral: One-page service menu (PDF) created, proposal template finalized, email templates created (kickoff, design preview, launch, etc.), pricing calculator or quote generator (optional, spreadsheet is fine).

Operational Systems: Trello board set up (or Notion), project folder template created (Google Drive), time tracking system decided (Toggl or spreadsheet), client intake form created (Google Form), pre-launch QA checklist (from Operations chapter).

Marketing & Sales: Google Business Profile created for your agency, LinkedIn profile updated (showcase web dev skills), Instagram business account created (optional), partner has list of warm leads to contact, referral program decided (10% off for referrals).

Financial Tracking: Separate bank account or clear tracking system, accounting software (Wave free) or spreadsheet, invoice numbering system, expense tracking system, budget for Year 1 operating costs ($100–300).

Skills & Knowledge: Comfortable building WordPress sites with Elementor, know how to install and configure essential plugins, can add basic SEO (meta tags, sitemap), can troubleshoot common WordPress issues, comfortable using Claude Code in Cursor for help, understand client onboarding process, know how to handle scope creep and difficult clients.

Month-by-Month Action Plan

Month 1: Foundation. Set up all tools and accounts. Build 1 portfolio project. Launch your agency website. Register business (or plan to).

Month 2: Portfolio Building. Build 2 more portfolio projects (real or fictional). Partner starts outreach to warm network. Create all sales collateral. Set up operational systems (Trello, etc.).

Month 3: First Paid Clients. Target: 2–3 projects (landing pages or Starter packages). Focus: deliver quality, get testimonials. Track: time spent per task (learn your speed). Refine: processes based on learnings.

Months 4–6: Scaling Up. Target: 3–4 projects per month. Start: retainer conversations with past clients. Optimize: templates, checklists, workflows. Build: referral momentum.

Months 7–9: Stabilizing. Target: 4–5 projects per month plus 5–6 retainers. Focus: quality over quantity. Consider: raising prices 15–20%. Systemize: everything that's repetitive.

Months 10–12: Growing. Target: 5–7 projects per month plus 8–10 retainers. Evaluate: hiring contractor if overwhelmed. Plan: Year 2 strategy (pricing, services, scaling). Celebrate: you've built a real business!

Success Metrics

Track these monthly: Projects (number of inquiries received, conversion rate inquiries to signed clients, average project value, number of projects completed, number of projects in pipeline). Financial (gross revenue, your share 50%, total expenses, net profit, effective hourly rate, revenue per project type). Efficiency (average time per project type, time spent on billable vs non-billable work, percentage of time on scope creep target less than 5%, number of revision rounds per project target 1–2). Client satisfaction (number of testimonials received, referrals received target 1 per 3 projects, repeat clients coming back for more work, client retention rate retainers staying active). Business health (cash flow money coming in vs going out, accounts receivable unpaid invoices, project completion rate finishing on time, portfolio pieces available target 8–10 by Month 6).

"On Track" Benchmarks: Month 3: 3+ projects completed, $800–1,200 revenue to you, 5+ portfolio pieces, 1–2 testimonials. Month 6: 12–15 projects completed (cumulative), $2,000–3,000 revenue to you, 2–4 retainer clients, 8+ portfolio pieces, effective hourly rate: $20–25. Month 12: 35–45 projects completed (cumulative), $30,000–40,000 revenue to you (cumulative Year 1), 6–10 retainer clients, effective hourly rate: $30–40, can confidently build any small business site, streamlined processes.

The Complete Cursor Prompt

This is the prompt you'll use in Cursor to have Claude Code help you build client websites. Copy-paste this into Cursor whenever starting a new project. 

# PROJECT: [CLIENT NAME] - WordPress Website Build

## CONTEXT
I'm a junior web developer building a WordPress website for a local business client. I'm using:
- LocalWP for local development
- WordPress 6.4+ (latest stable)
- Astra theme (free version) or GeneratePress (free)
- Elementor page builder (free version)
- Essential plugins: Rank Math SEO, Wordfence, UpdraftPlus, WPForms
- Performance: LiteSpeed Cache (free, if host supports) or WP Super Cache; image optimization (e.g. Smush or ShortPixel free tier) if needed to hit load targets

## CLIENT DETAILS
- **Business Name:** [CLIENT BUSINESS NAME]
- **Industry:** [e.g., HVAC contractor, restaurant, real estate]
- **Location/City:** [e.g., Hamilton, ON — for local SEO and GBP]
- **Target Audience:** [e.g., homeowners in Hamilton, ON seeking plumbing services]
- **Website Goals:** [e.g., generate leads via contact form, show up in Google searches, appear professional]

## PROJECT SCOPE
- **Pages:** [List all pages, e.g., Home, About, Services, Gallery, Contact]
- **Key Features:**
  - [e.g., Contact form]
  - [e.g., Google Maps integration]
  - [e.g., Image gallery]
  - [e.g., Mobile responsive design]
- **Hosting:** [e.g., SiteGround, Hostinger, or TBD — affects caching options]
- **Timeline:** [X] business days
- **Budget:** $[AMOUNT] (client pays, I need to deliver efficiently)

## CURRENT TASK
[Describe what you're working on right now]

Example:
"I need to build the homepage. It should have:
1. Hero section with headline, subheadline, CTA button
2. Services section (3 columns showcasing main services)
3. Testimonials section (2-3 client reviews)
4. CTA section (call-to-action to contact)
5. Footer with contact info and social links"

If I'm stuck: I'll paste the exact error message or describe what I see vs what I expect.

## WHAT I NEED FROM YOU
Please help me:
1. [Specific question or task]
2. [Another specific question or task]
3. [etc.]

## MY SKILL LEVEL
- I know basic HTML, CSS, JavaScript, Python, PHP
- I'm comfortable reading code but not expert at writing from scratch
- I use Cursor/Claude daily — you can suggest custom code (Custom HTML, CSS, or snippets) when Elementor can't do something
- I'm learning WordPress, Elementor, and web development best practices
- Please explain technical concepts briefly when you introduce them

## CONSTRAINTS
- Must use free plugins only (no premium unless absolutely necessary)
- Prefer Astra/Elementor built-in options over adding extra plugins where possible
- Must load in under 3 seconds (performance is critical — suggest caching and image optimization)
- Must be mobile-responsive (majority of traffic is mobile)
- Must be client-maintainable (they should be able to edit text/images easily)

## EXPECTED OUTPUT FORMAT
When providing code:
- Include brief comments explaining what the code does
- Tell me exactly WHERE to add the code (which file, which section, or in Elementor: which widget and which tab)
- Explain WHY this approach (not just WHAT)

When providing instructions (especially for Elementor):
- Step-by-step (numbered list)
- Use exact widget names and panel tabs (Content, Style, Advanced) so I can find the settings
- Include screenshots or exact menu paths where possible
- Warn me about common mistakes to avoid

## ADDITIONAL NOTES
[Any other relevant information about the project, client preferences, technical challenges, etc.]

How to Use This Prompt: Copy the template above. Fill in the bracketed sections with your specific project details. Paste into Cursor whenever you start a new website or need help with a task. Update "CURRENT TASK" section as you work through the project. Ask follow-up questions as needed — Claude Code remembers context.

Final Words of Advice

1. Start Scrappy, Scale Smart. You don't need everything perfect to start. You need 3 portfolio pieces (even if fictional), a way to accept payment, a contract template, your skills plus AI tools. Launch with that. Improve as you go.

2. AI Tools Are Your Superpower. Claude Code in Cursor is a force multiplier. Use it liberally: ask it to review your code, ask it to explain concepts you don't understand, ask it to generate boilerplate, ask it to debug errors, ask it "Why?" questions to learn. This is how you compete with experienced developers despite being junior.

3. Under-Promise, Over-Deliver. If you think it'll take 2 weeks, say 3 weeks. If you think you can do 5 features, promise 3. Clients LOVE when you deliver early and add bonuses. They HATE when you're late, even if quality is great.

4. Your Reputation Is Everything. Especially early on: be honest about your experience level ("I'm a junior dev but I use cutting-edge AI tools to deliver quality work fast"), deliver on time even if imperfect (you can polish later), respond quickly to emails (within 24 hours), fix mistakes immediately and without complaint, ask for testimonials and referrals (they're your best marketing).

5. Track Your Time Religiously. Even though you charge per project, track time: you'll learn where time goes, you'll get better at estimating, you'll know if you're profitable, you'll identify bottlenecks to optimize. Target: $25–40/hour effective rate by Month 6.

6. Say No to Bad Clients. Red flags: haggling excessively on price, wanting to skip the deposit, vague scope ("I'll know it when I see it"), rushed timeline ("I need this in 3 days"), rude or condescending in initial conversations. It's better to have no client than a nightmare client.

7. Your Partner Relationship Matters. Communicate openly and often. Have weekly check-ins. Celebrate wins together. Address issues immediately (don't let resentment build). Be flexible but have boundaries. Written agreement on split and roles.

8. Invest in Yourself. As soon as you can afford it: professional liability insurance ($300–600/year), paid tools that save time (WP Rocket, premium themes), courses/books that level up skills, networking events (local business groups), your own mental health (burnout is real).

9. You WILL Make Mistakes. You'll underestimate timelines. You'll have bugs go live. You'll have a client not pay. You'll accidentally delete something important. You'll promise something you can't deliver. This is normal. Learn from each one. Document what went wrong and how you'll avoid it next time. The fact that you're reading this guide shows you're preparing well.

10. The First Year Is About Learning, Not Just Earning. Yes, you need money. But you also need portfolio pieces, testimonials, processes and systems, technical skills, business skills (sales, project management, client relations), confidence. If you end Year 1 with $30K in your pocket AND the skills/systems to do $80K+ in Year 2, you've won.
