## 9. RISK MANAGEMENT

### 9.1 Common Risks & Mitigation Strategies

**RISK 1: Not Enough Clients**

**Likelihood:** High (Months 1-3)  
**Impact:** High (no revenue)

**Mitigation:**
- Build 3 portfolio pieces BEFORE officially launching
- Offer discounted projects to friends/family/network
- Partner focuses on lead generation while you build portfolio
- Have 6 months of personal expenses saved (if possible)
- Keep part-time job until Month 3-6 (you mentioned you have one)

**Early Warning Signs:**
- Month 3: Less than 2 inquiries per week
- Month 6: Less than 2 projects closed per month

**Action Plan:**
- Adjust pricing (temporarily lower if needed)
- Increase partner's outreach efforts
- Run limited-time promotions
- Expand service area (beyond GTA if needed)

---

**RISK 2: Projects Take Longer Than Expected**

**Likelihood:** High (Months 1-6, you're learning)  
**Impact:** Medium (low effective hourly rate, unhappy clients)

**Mitigation:**
- Use AI tools aggressively (Claude Code, ChatGPT)
- Create templates and reusable components
- Add 30% buffer to your internal timeline estimates
- Be honest with clients about being a junior dev (builds trust)
- Under-promise, over-deliver on timelines

**Early Warning Signs:**
- Projects consistently taking 2x your estimate
- Clients asking "When will it be done?"
- You're working 50+ hours/week and falling behind

**Action Plan:**
- Analyze where time is going (track meticulously)
- Automate or template repetitive tasks
- Ask Claude Code to help optimize your workflow
- Consider raising prices to reduce volume (more time per project)
- Pause new projects until current ones are complete

---

**RISK 3: Scope Creep (Clients Want More Than Agreed)**

**Likelihood:** High (especially with new clients)  
**Impact:** Medium (reduces profitability, delays)

**Mitigation:**
- Very clear scope in proposal and contract
- "Unlimited minor revisions" clearly defined (text edits, image swaps)
- Major changes = new quote (in writing)
- Use project management tool (Trello) to track approved scope
- Politely but firmly say "That's outside the current scope, but I can quote it separately"

**Early Warning Signs:**
- Client asking for "just one more page"
- Revisions expanding beyond minor changes
- Requests for features not in original proposal

**Action Plan:**
- Reference the original proposal: "In our agreement we specified [X]. What you're asking for is [Y], which I'm happy to do for an additional $[amount]."
- Document everything in email (paper trail)
- Be kind but firm
- Offer options: add to current project (pay more) OR add after launch (separate project)

---

**RISK 4: Non-Paying Clients**

**Likelihood:** Medium  
**Impact:** High (waste of time, loss of income)

**Mitigation:**
- ALWAYS get 50% deposit before starting
- Deposits are non-refundable (in contract)
- Final 50% due before site goes live on their domain
- Keep site on staging subdomain until paid
- Use payment processors (Stripe) for card-on-file
- Clear payment terms in contract (Net 15)

**Early Warning Signs:**
- Client delays paying deposit
- Client doesn't respond to final invoice
- Client disputes work quality after completion

**Action Plan:**
- Follow payment policy strictly (see Part 1)
- Take site offline if payment not received by Day 45
- Small claims court if necessary (for amounts >$1,000)
- Learn from it: tighten contract, better client qualification

---

**RISK 5: Technical Issues (Site Hacked, Data Loss, Downtime)**

**Likelihood:** Low-Medium  
**Impact:** High (reputation damage, client trust)

**Mitigation:**
- Security plugin on every site (Wordfence)
- Weekly automated backups (UpdraftPlus to Google Drive)
- Test backup restoration (at least once)
- SSL certificate on every site (HTTPS)
- Strong passwords, limit login attempts
- Update WordPress, themes, plugins regularly
- Uptime monitoring (UptimeRobot free)

**Early Warning Signs:**
- Client reports site is slow or down
- Wordfence alerts about suspicious activity
- Google flags site as "unsafe"

**Action Plan:**
- Immediate response (within 2 hours for critical issues)
- Restore from backup if site compromised
- Scan with Wordfence, clean malware
- Change all passwords
- Notify client immediately and honestly
- If your fault: offer refund or free month of maintenance

---

**RISK 6: Partner Relationship Issues**

**Likelihood:** Medium  
**Impact:** High (business could collapse)

**Mitigation:**
- Clear written agreement on revenue split (50/50)
- Define roles clearly (you build, they sell)
- Regular check-ins (weekly 30-min calls)
- Transparent communication on financials
- Separate business bank account (split deposits)
- Written partnership agreement (even if informal initially)

**Early Warning Signs:**
- Disagreements on pricing or scope
- One partner not pulling their weight
- Money disputes
- Poor communication

**Action Plan:**
- Address issues immediately (don't let resentment build)
- Revisit agreement (is it still fair as business grows?)
- Consider mediation if serious conflict
- Have exit clause in partnership agreement (how to split if it ends)

### 9.2 Insurance & Legal Protection

**Types of Insurance to Consider:**

**Professional Liability Insurance (Errors & Omissions)**
- **What it covers:** Mistakes, missed deadlines, negligence claims
- **Cost:** $300-600/year for $1M coverage
- **When to get:** Before your 5th client or $10K in revenue
- **Recommended providers:** Simply Business, Hiscox, thimble

**General Liability Insurance**
- **What it covers:** Bodily injury, property damage (less relevant for web dev)
- **Cost:** $200-400/year
- **When to get:** If meeting clients in person or at their location
- **Often bundled with professional liability**

**Cyber Liability Insurance**
- **What it covers:** Data breaches, cyber attacks on client sites
- **Cost:** $500-1,000/year
- **When to get:** If handling sensitive client data (e-commerce, medical)

**Recommendation:** Start with **professional liability only** once you hit $10K revenue or 5 clients. Defer others until Year 2.

---

**Legal Contracts (Essential):**

**Client Service Agreement (Contract)**
- Scope of work
- Payment terms
- Revision policy
- Ownership of work
- Limitation of liability
- Termination clause

**Where to get:**
- Law Depot (Canada-specific templates, ~$50)
- Legal Zoom
- Local lawyer (consultation + custom contract: $500-1,000)

**Start with:** Template from Law Depot, have lawyer review once you can afford it.

### 10.2 When & How to Hire

**DON'T hire too early.** Common mistake.

**When to consider hiring:**
- ✅ You're consistently turning away work (good problem)
- ✅ You're working 50+ hours/week for 3+ months
- ✅ You have 10+ retainer clients (need support)
- ✅ Revenue is $8,000+/month consistently (can afford it)

**Who to hire first:**

**Option 1: Junior Developer (Part-Time Contractor)**
- **Role:** Build sites under your direction
- **Cost:** $20-30/hour CAD, 10-20 hours/week
- **When:** You're booked solid with projects

**Option 2: Virtual Assistant (VA)**
- **Role:** Client communication, scheduling, admin tasks
- **Cost:** $15-25/hour CAD, 10-15 hours/week
- **When:** You're spending >10 hours/week on admin

**Option 3: SEO Specialist (Contractor)**
- **Role:** Handle all SEO work (you just build sites)
- **Cost:** $25-40/hour CAD or revenue share
- **When:** You have 5+ SEO retainer clients

**Recommendation:** Start with VA to free up your time, then hire dev if volume justifies it.

```markdown
## CONTEXT
I'm a junior web developer building a WordPress website for a local business client. I'm using:
- LocalWP for local development

- Astra theme (free version)
- Elementor page builder (free version)
- Essential plugins: Rank Math SEO, Wordfence, UpdraftPlus, WPForms

## CLIENT DETAILS
- **Business Name:** [CLIENT BUSINESS NAME]
- **Industry:** [e.g., HVAC contractor, restaurant, real estate]
- **Target Audience:** [e.g., homeowners in Hamilton, ON seeking plumbing services]
- **Website Goals:** [e.g., generate leads via contact form, show up in Google searches, appear professional]

## PROJECT SCOPE
- **Pages:** [List all pages, e.g., Home, About, Services, Gallery, Contact]
- **Key Features:**
  - [e.g., Contact form]
  - [e.g., Google Maps integration]
  - [e.g., Image gallery]
  - [e.g., Mobile responsive design]
- **Timeline:** [X] business days
- **Budget:** $[AMOUNT] (client pays, I need to deliver efficiently)

## CURRENT TASK
[Describe what you're working on right now]

Example:
"I need to build the homepage. It should have:
1. Hero section with headline, subheadline, CTA button
2. Services section (3 columns showcasing main services)
3. Testimonials section (2-3 client reviews)
4. CTA section (call-to-action to contact)
5. Footer with contact info and social links"

## WHAT I NEED FROM YOU
Please help me:
1. [Specific question or task]
2. [Another specific question or task]
3. [etc.]

## MY SKILL LEVEL
- I know basic HTML, CSS, JavaScript, Python, PHP
- I'm comfortable reading code but not expert at writing from scratch
- I'm learning WordPress, Elementor, and web development best practices
- Please explain technical concepts briefly when you introduce them

## CONSTRAINTS
- Must use free plugins only (no premium unless absolutely necessary)
- Must load in under 3 seconds (performance is critical)
- Must be mobile-responsive (majority of traffic is mobile)
- Must be client-maintainable (they should be able to edit text/images easily)

## EXPECTED OUTPUT FORMAT
When providing code:
- Include brief comments explaining what the code does
- Tell me exactly WHERE to add the code (which file, which section)
- Explain WHY this approach (not just WHAT)

When providing instructions:
- Step-by-step (numbered list)
- Include screenshots or exact menu paths where possible
- Warn me about common mistakes to avoid

## ADDITIONAL NOTES
[Any other relevant information about the project, client preferences, technical challenges, etc.]
```

**How to Use This Prompt:**

1. **Copy the template above**
2. **Fill in the bracketed sections** with your specific project details
3. **Paste into Cursor** whenever you start a new website or need help with a task
4. **Update "CURRENT TASK"** section as you work through the project
5. **Ask follow-up questions** as needed—Claude Code remembers context
